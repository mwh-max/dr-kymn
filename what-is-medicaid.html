What's Medicaid?

What is Medicaid, really?

Medicaid is a public health coverage program for people with lower incomes. Here’s what it is, who it’s for, and how Kentucky runs it — in plain language.

The short version

Medicaid is a joint federal and state program that pays for health care for people who qualify based on income (and sometimes age, disability, or family status). In Kentucky, it covers children, pregnant people, parents and caretaker relatives, adults, seniors, and people with disabilities who meet the requirements.

Who runs it in Kentucky?

The Kentucky Department for Medicaid Services (DMS), part of the Cabinet for Health and Family Services (CHFS), sets the rules and pays for the program. Most members get their care through a private Medicaid managed care organization (MCO) — a health plan the state contracts with — rather than directly from the state.

Medicaid vs. Medicare

These are often confused, but they’re different programs. Medicaid is based on income and is run jointly by the state and federal government. Medicare is a federal program mainly for people 65 and older or with certain disabilities, and isn’t income-based. Some people qualify for both at the same time.

Medicaid vs. KCHIP

KCHIP is Kentucky’s children’s health insurance program, for kids under 19 in families that earn a bit too much for Medicaid. kynect checks both programs automatically when you apply, so you don’t need to know in advance which one fits. More on KCHIP →

What does it cover?

Doctor visits, hospital care, prescriptions, mental health and substance use treatment, and more — plus dental and vision for children. Exact benefits depend on your MCO plan. See covered services →

What does it cost?

For most members there’s little to no premium, and copays — if any — are low. Costs can vary by program and household income.
